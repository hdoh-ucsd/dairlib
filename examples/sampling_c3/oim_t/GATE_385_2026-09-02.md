## Defect

`BuildXarmFullSamplingC3CorridorSafePrefix` truncates
`execution_state_trajectory` when it is non-empty and rewrites the terminal
positions, but leaves `dynamic_state_trajectory` at full horizon. The
progress path's `candidate_terminal_pose` decoded
`dynamic_state_trajectory.back()`, so yaw progress, terminal descent,
component transactions, and response conditioning were ranked on the
full-horizon plan while lateral/y predictions read the truncated prefix end.
Measured consequences (run `xarm6_gate384b_...b12e63`): 103 of 135 corridor
evaluations retain 1 of 6 knots; the selected rotation candidate promised
0.357 rad yaw with a -24 mm translation trade and the executed prefix
delivered 0.069 rad with positive translation — the promised trade never
existed, but the admission machinery reasoned about it, and its accrued
lateral drift then hit the strict recovery gate.

## Change

`candidate_terminal_pose` selects the same trajectory the prefix builder
truncates (execution when non-empty, else dynamic) and decodes its
terminal. No solver setting, cost, tolerance, seed, or acceptance threshold
changed; the predicted-capsule-clearance sweep intentionally still checks
the full-horizon states (stricter is safe).

## Validation

`results/xarm6_gate385_prefixterm_exact8000_77a76a` (+ D mirror + video,
final frame verified): 7 cycles, all positive on translation AND yaw;
individual cycle yaw up to 0.147 rad (2-4x prior cycles); total yaw
progress 0.546 rad and terminal orientation 2.5957 rad — both the best of
any run to date (Gate-380 baseline: 0.471 / 2.670). Terminal
`open_table_success` still FAIL (0.734971 m / 2.5957 rad) at the parked
recovery-admission policy wall: the run's accrued 3.4 mm translation debt
uniformly rejects all 128 executable recovery candidates (identical reject
numbers across candidates prove it is measured-state policy, not a
prediction leak).
